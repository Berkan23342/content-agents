# Outbreak Diary: Dead Air, story bible

The one place that remembers this story. The writer reads it before every part and updates **Current state** and
**Episode log** after writing one. Everything here is original and fictional.

## The world

- **Port Ellis**, the same rainy harbour city as the other Outbreak Diary stories, on the same night. Never name real
  cities, brands or people.
- **Gull Hill Radio**, a small station in a tower on Gull Hill above the city. Studio, a hallway, a stairwell, a roof
  with an old mast.
- **The roof speakers:** forty old civil-defence siren speakers, wired to the studio microphone. Whatever Wren says on
  air is shouted over the whole city. The speaker switch is dead after Day 2; the mast still broadcasts.
- **The school** at the bottom of Gull Hill, where a child hides.
- **Harbor Station** is on the radio, saying boats leave Pier Four at dawn.

## The infected ("them")

Same rules as every Outbreak Diary story:

- They hunt by sound. Anything loud draws them; Wren's amplified voice is the worst of all.
- They are clumsy on stairs and cannot open doors with handles.
- They are slow by day and restless at night.
- A bite brings fever, then silence, then change.
- Rain hides small sounds.

## People

- **Wren (the narrator)**, thirty, the overnight host of Gull Hill Radio. Warm, dry, used to talking to strangers in
  the dark. She tells the story like a broadcast.
- **Tilly**, about nine, hiding in a classroom at the school and calling the studio phone. Speaks in a whisper.
- **Cal**, the station's engineer, a man in his forties who is somewhere on the mast and voices live on air.
  Careful, tired, hides something.

## Season plan (ends on Day 12)

The story ends on Day 12 (story/series.json `finale`); after that its slot goes to a new story.

- **Days 1-4.** The speakers, Cal, and getting Tilly out of the school with silence.
- **Days 5-9.** Other callers, a rescue run down the hill, someone using the mast to lure the infected, Cal's secret.
- **Days 10-11.** A plan to use the speakers one last time: a sound that sends the city away from the harbour.
- **Day 12, the finale.** Wren speaks one last time, takes Tilly and Cal to the boats and ends the broadcast on
  one strong image (the ON AIR light going dark at dawn). No cliffhanger that needs a Day 13.

## Voices

- The narrator is always the woman telling the story.
- A character can say a line in their own voice: put the spoken words alone in a beat with `"speaker"`.
- Speakers in this story: `tilly` (a girl), `cal` (a man), `announcer` (the Harbor Station radio voice). Anyone else is
  quoted by the narrator.
- Use one or two spoken lines per part.

## Rules for every part

- **One part is one day.** Part N is Day N; never open with "Day N."
- 100-125 words, 8-14 beats; the first line a hook, the last a cliffhanger.
- First person, past tense, short sentences. Write like a broadcast: Wren can address listeners.
- PG-13: tension and danger, never gore, no swearing, animals never die.
- **Scenes to use:** `radio` (the studio), `rooftop`, `hallway`, `stairwell`, `phone`, `window`, `door`, `supplies`,
  `kitchen`, plus trips: `school`, `supermarket`, `hospital`, `gas_station`, `truck`, `lobby`, `subway`. Never use
  `building`, `room` or `peephole`.

## Current state

- **Who:** Wren alone; Tilly in the school; Cal's voice on the mast.
- **Supplies:** wire cutters, torch, tape.

## Episode log

- **Part 1 (Day 1) The Studio Phone:** Wren hears the studio phone ring. Tilly whispers from a school classroom. Wren whispers back, and every speaker on the roof repeats it.
- **Part 2 (Day 2) Roof Speakers:** the street climbs the hill. Wren cuts the roof cable and the speakers die. A man's voice, Cal, says she cut the wrong one.
