# Outbreak Diary: Route 9, story bible

The one place that remembers this story. The writer reads it before every part and updates **Current state** and
**Episode log** after writing one. Everything here is original and fictional.

## The world

- **Port Ellis** and the hills around it, the same rainy region as the other Outbreak Diary stories, on the same
  outbreak. Never name real places, brands or people.
- **Bus 9**, an old yellow school bus with a dented bumper, a loud engine and a horn that carries for a mile. Nobody
  knows exactly where the other buses went.
- **Route 9** was a school run from the hill school out through Cedar Flats to Port Ellis. The school is behind
  them now. The goal: reach Pier Four, where Harbor Station says boats leave at dawn.
- Fuel is the clock: the gauge shows a quarter tank.

## The infected ("them")

Same rules as every Outbreak Diary story:

- They hunt by sound.
- They are clumsy and cannot open doors with handles.
- They are slow by day and restless at night.
- A bite brings fever, then silence, then change.
- **Here:** the engine and the horn call them from far away. A parked, silent bus is almost invisible; a moving bus
  gathers a tail. Closed windows and low gears keep it quiet.

## People

- **Mara (the narrator)**, thirty-six, the bus driver. Calm, practical, a little funny, writes her journal on the back
  of her route sheets. She promised every parent she would bring their child home.
- **Ollie**, ten, the youngest and the one who counts everything out loud. Brave, small, speaks his mind.
- **Dell**, a man in his fifties, a mechanic she picks up on Day 3 or so. Gruff, honest, knows the engine.
- **The kids**, six on the bus at the start: Ollie, Priya, Theo, Juno, Beck and little Sol. Only Ollie speaks often.

## Season plan (ends on Day 10)

The story ends on Day 10 (story/series.json `finale`); after that its slot goes to a new story.

- **Days 1-3.** The bus leaves the school; the kids, the rules of the bus (never stop, never honk) and a first
  roadblock. Fuel low.
- **Days 4-7.** A gas station, a supermarket, a stranger, a sick child scare (not a bite), a bridge.
- **Days 8-9.** The last stretch to the city: the harbour road is full, one more plan.
- **Day 10, the finale.** The bus reaches Pier Four. Mara hands the children over, the last horn call draws the dead
  away, and she ends on one quiet image: the empty yellow bus on the pier at dawn. No cliffhanger that needs a Day 11.

## Voices

- The narrator is always the woman telling the story.
- A character can say a line in their own voice: put the spoken words alone in a beat with `"speaker"`.
- Speakers in this story: `ollie` (a boy), `dell` (a man), `announcer` (the Harbor Station radio voice). Anyone else
  is quoted by the narrator.
- Use one or two spoken lines per part.

## Rules for every part

- **One part is one day.** Part N is Day N; never open with "Day N."
- 100-125 words, 8-14 beats; the first line a hook, the last a cliffhanger.
- First person, past tense, short sentences. A journal: Mara writes short dated entries on route sheets.
- PG-13: tension and danger, never gore, no swearing, animals never die.
- **Scenes to use:** `subway` (the inside of the bus, with `moving`), `truck` (the road ahead through the
  windscreen), `phone`, `radio`, `supplies`, `door`, `lobby`, plus stops: `gas_station`, `supermarket`, `school`,
  `hospital`, `skybridge`, `lake`, `street`, `forest`. Never use `building`, `room` or `peephole`.

## Episode log
- **Part 1 (Day 1) The Last Bus:** Mara drives six children out of the silent school. The engine and horn call the dead, the road ahead is a wall of cars, and Sol starts to cry.
- **Part 2 (Day 2) Quiet Gears:** Mara hides the bus in a ditch, raids the supermarket and meets Dell, who says the muffler is cracked. Thirty shapes walk out of the fog; Ollie whispers not to start the engine.
